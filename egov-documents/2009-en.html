Recommendations of the participants in E-Governance Day 2009

National Academy of Public Administration under the President of Ukraine · 14 May 2009

Source Original participant recommendations held in the event archive

To the Verkhovna Rada, the Cabinet of Ministers and the Presidential Secretariat

  1. Develop a national framework for implementing e-governance in Ukraine.
  2. Develop national strategies for implementing e-democracy and e-governance.
  3. Initiate a dedicated national implementation programme.
  4. Accelerate legislation and targeted programmes so that e-governance can develop despite limited resources, especially funding.
  5. Engage all public authorities. Coordinate activities and timing with public administration reform, including civil-service responsibility, accountability, transparency and delegation, and with anti-corruption programmes.
  6. Provide for national monitoring of civil servants’ performance as part of quality management. Recommend publishing performance rankings with the underlying standards and criteria, adapted to work in an e-governance environment.

To the Verkhovna Rada

  1. Develop bills and adapt existing legal instruments, including those on personal information and information.
  2. Improve public procurement legislation in relation to e-governance.
  3. Develop and amend rules governing communications policy in support of e-democracy.
  4. Create a regulatory framework for public-private partnerships on e-governance projects.
  5. Provide for electronic agreements in the Civil, Commercial, Criminal and other codes.
  6. Amend the Law on Citizens’ Appeals to address electronic submissions.
  7. Adopt legal instruments establishing common IT approaches, technologies and standards.
  8. Harmonize e-governance regulatory documentation and standards.
  9. Improve public administration lawmaking and promote public legal literacy.

To the Cabinet, the State Committee for Informatization, and central and local executive authorities

  1. Have the Cabinet initiate a draft strategy for developing the information society.
  2. Draft an e-governance development strategy for Ukraine.
  3. Implement e-governance as an integrated set of software solutions.
  4. Create a national implementation programme and develop sectoral programmes.
  5. Include a dedicated e-governance section in the National Informatization Programme.
  6. Develop and implement national information exchange standards and protocols, including for information security and protection.
  7. Have the Cabinet complete the national information society indicator system by the end of 2009.
  8. Increase the 2010 budget for informatization of public authorities.
  9. Assign national responsibility to a deputy prime minister and designate deputy heads in central and local authorities.
  10. Place implementation at Cabinet level under a lead of at least deputy prime minister rank, with two deputies for public administration and technology. Involve the State Service of Special Communications and Information Protection in addressing information protection.
  11. Establish units responsible for implementation within executive authorities.
  12. Establish distinct units in central and local executive authorities and local governments, preferably reporting to the head or deputy, to coordinate and support projects and provide information and analytical support.
  13. Establish a Ministry for Information Society Development.
  14. Designate one authority for communications policy and e-democracy. Consider assigning the functions to the State Committee for Television and Radio Broadcasting and renaming it if appropriate.
  15. Monitor Ukrainian e-governance programmes and projects.
  16. Develop, approve and publish readiness indicators for public bodies and local governments.
  17. Approve a list of services to deliver electronically, giving priority to users’ needs.
  18. Coordinate implementation nationally and integrate existing resources using common data standards and shared databases.
  19. Use mobile government technologies widely to expand access to public information and services.
  20. Organize activities that promote e-governance.
  21. Encourage the public to use digital tools when accessing government services.
  22. Create a nationwide network of free public internet access points.
  23. Create a single government portal for electronic services.
  24. Develop electronic public services and standardize their delivery processes.
  25. Define e-democracy standards.
  26. Introduce central and regional electronic document management systems and public access points through municipal, town and village councils and educational institutions.
  27. Set up a joint commission to align implementation standards with European standards.
  28. Create an interdepartmental working group to coordinate implementation and make decisions.
  29. Expand media coverage of Ukrainian e-governance as part of wider European e-governance.
  30. Establish national conditions for the use of e-governance technologies.
  31. Resolve digital signature certification and use within executive authorities, including oblast administrations, to enable fully electronic document workflows.
  32. Give greater attention to informatization in cities, villages, towns and urban districts.
  33. Compare developments in 2008 and 2009.
  34. Help local executive authorities, particularly at district level, implement e-governance technologies.
  35. Establish implementation offices.
  36. Secure funding to implement the recommendations.
  37. The Cabinet and central and local executive authorities should provide each citizen with electronic identification funded from the state budget, develop standard central and local electronic document systems for rapid, affordable adoption, translate, publish and implement Council of Europe Recommendation CM/Rec(2009)1 on e-democracy, and develop electronic tools for preparing government and government committee meetings.
  38. Present local implementation experience.
  39. Accelerate the development and approval of document workflow and digital signature formats and rules.
  40. Expand the use of electronic signatures.
  41. Approve a Ukrainian electronic passport compatible with widely used software in EU countries.
  42. Expedite the personal data bill and draft and adopt legislation on electronic commerce.
  43. Resolve funding for the central certification authority (TsZO).
  44. Database system [brief wording in the source].
  45. Select an existing city-level geographic information system from an oblast or district city for a pilot with potential national rollout.
  46. Expedite the electronic document exchange system (SEDO).
  47. Develop and introduce tools enabling civil servants to answer citizens’ electronic submissions.
  48. Compile a glossary of key e-governance terms.
  49. Have the State Committee for Informatization align legislation and regulations with current international requirements and develop common interfaces for electronic document exchange.
  50. Have the Committee build on Vinnytsia City Council’s work, standardize it and implement it in all city councils.
  51. Fund regional informatization through the national programme and address district staffing and regional representation on advisory bodies.
  52. Have the Committee, the Cabinet Secretariat, the Verkhovna Rada Office, the Academy, civil society and IT companies jointly develop a survey on the state and prospects of e-governance in government bodies.

To the National Academy of Public Administration

  1. Hold E-Governance Day 2010 on 28 April 2010 in connection with World Telecommunication and Information Society Day.
  2. Publish the event materials on the Information Policy Department’s website.
  3. Involve the Verkhovna Rada’s Informatization Advisory Council.
  4. Broaden participation by political parties and civic associations at all levels.
  5. Expand the event with participation from many Ukrainian e-governance systems integrators.
  6. Hold regional E-Governance Days hosted by public authorities.
  7. Conduct research at the Academy and its regional public administration institutes.
  8. Prepare implementation guidance and manuals for central, oblast, municipal and village levels.
  9. Consider creating an Institute of E-Governance.
  10. Train civil servants in ICT for e-governance and e-democracy.
  11. Ask the Ministry of Education and Science to develop media education curricula for all ages.
  12. Add the qualification “manager of e-governance organizational systems” to the Classification of Occupations, and train specialists through the Academy’s E-Governance programme.
  13. Create an E-Governance teaching and research centre within the Information Policy Department.
  14. Deliver professional development in e-governance for civil servants.
  15. Introduce initial or continuing training for implementation and use specialists.
  16. Organize round tables and seminars on e-governance for senior central executive officials.
  17. Work with leading IT companies to organize one- to two-week courses for executive managers and IT specialists.
  18. Finish equipping the e-governance classroom and hold a formal opening.

To companies working in e-governance

  1. Invite software developers to deploy e-governance technologies in state and local government bodies.
  2. Consider allowing businesses to take part under state guarantees, with payment after systems are operational, including through local and district budgets.
