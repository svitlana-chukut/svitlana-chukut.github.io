Recommendations of E-Governance Day 2008

National Academy of Public Administration under the President of Ukraine · 14 May 2008

Source Published conference proceedings, pp. 126–128

To the Verkhovna Rada, the Presidential Secretariat and the Cabinet of Ministers

  1. Develop a nationwide framework for implementing e-governance in Ukraine.
  2. Develop national strategies for e-democracy and e-governance.
  3. Initiate a dedicated national implementation programme.

To the Verkhovna Rada

  1. Draft bills and adapt legislation and regulations on e-governance, including laws on personal information and information.
  2. Improve Ukrainian public procurement legislation in relation to e-governance.
  3. Draft and amend legal provisions for communications policy in the context of e-democracy.
  4. Establish a regulatory framework for public-private partnerships on informatization projects.
  5. Adopt legal instruments establishing common IT approaches, technologies and standards.
  6. Provide for electronic agreements in the Civil, Commercial, Criminal and other codes.
  7. Amend the Law on Citizens’ Appeals to provide for processing electronic submissions.

To the Cabinet and central and local executive authorities

  1. Include a dedicated e-governance section in the National Informatization Programme.
  2. Assign national responsibility to the relevant deputy prime minister and designate deputy heads in central and local authorities.
  3. Create dedicated IT units in central and local executive authorities and local governments, preferably reporting directly to the head or deputy. They should coordinate cooperation, implement and support projects, and provide information and analytical support.
  4. Monitor e-governance programmes and projects in Ukraine.
  5. Develop, approve and publish national indicators of public authorities’ and local governments’ readiness.
  6. Approve a list of services for electronic delivery, giving priority to users’ needs.
  7. Coordinate implementation nationally and integrate existing resources using common data standards and shared databases.
  8. Use mobile government technologies widely to expand access to information and public services.
  9. Organize activities to promote e-governance.
  10. Encourage use of e-governance services and digital tools for obtaining public services.
  11. Create a nationwide network of free public internet access points.
  12. Create a single government portal for electronic services.
  13. Develop electronic public services and standardize their delivery processes.
  14. Define e-democracy standards.
  15. Introduce electronic document management and public access points at central and regional levels, based at city, town and village councils and educational institutions.
  16. Set up a joint commission to align implementation standards with European standards.
  17. Designate one authority for state communications policy, including e-democracy. Consider assigning the function to the State Committee for Television and Radio Broadcasting, with a name change if appropriate.
  18. Compile a glossary of key information and communications terms.
  19. Develop and deploy tools for civil servants to answer electronic submissions from citizens.
  20. Expand the use of electronic signatures.
  21. Publish the event materials on the government portal and the State Committee for Informatization’s website.

To the National Academy of Public Administration

  1. Hold E-Governance Day every year ahead of World Telecommunication and Information Society Day on 17 May.
  2. Recommend that higher education institutions offer a master’s programme in e-governance. Have the Academy develop one and a series of professional development courses.
  3. Document government IT experience in a handbook, guidance and online materials.
  4. Hold regular seminars and exchanges between public bodies’ IT administration units.
  5. Conduct research at the Academy and the regional public administration institutes.
  6. Prepare methodological guidance and manuals for implementation at central, oblast, municipal and village levels.
  7. Consider establishing an Institute of E-Governance.
  8. Train civil servants in ICT for e-governance and e-democracy.
  9. Introduce initial or continuing courses for specialists in implementing and using e-governance.
  10. Train senior central executive officials on the importance of e-governance through round tables and seminars.
  11. Organize one- to two-week courses for executive managers and IT specialists with leading IT companies.
  12. Open an e-governance classroom with support from Microsoft Ukraine.

The recommendations were sent to the Presidential Secretariat, the Cabinet Secretariat, the Office of the Verkhovna Rada, the Main Department of Civil Service and the State Committee for Informatization of Ukraine.
