Proposals for the 2011 draft Presidential Address on the information society

Historical working document

Source Proposals submitted for consideration in the draft Presidential Address

Context and assessment

A competitive economy is increasingly important for sustainable development and requires the wide adoption of information and communication technologies (ICT). ICT drives economic growth by helping develop industries, raise productivity, create jobs and strengthen Ukraine’s international standing.

ICT is part of everyday life and improves access to information for people, businesses and governments. Information is valuable because it helps create knowledge. Most countries prioritize a transition to a knowledge society. A central objective of ICT is therefore to help people acquire the knowledge and skills this transition requires.

Over the previous decade Ukraine laid the legal foundations for digital signatures, electronic document management, information protection and digital information resources. Parliament adopted the Law on the Basic Principles of Information Society Development in Ukraine for 2007–2015 in 2007, followed by an implementation plan. These instruments were intended to advance the information society and make information technology a policy priority.

The E-Governance Development Concept approved in 2010 identified e-governance as a tool for building the information society. E-governance is a way to organize public administration using ICT to make state bodies and local government more effective, open and transparent, and to build a state that responds to citizens’ needs. Its principal component is electronic government, a common infrastructure for information exchange among state and local authorities and with citizens and businesses.

At the time, all executive authorities had a web presence, and public bodies were improving the content of their sites in line with Cabinet Resolution No. 3 of 4 January 2002 on publishing information online about executive authorities’ work. Most departmental document systems registered incoming and outgoing documents and lacked full electronic workflows. A dedicated network transmitted confidential information between executive authorities under the law on the national confidential communications system. Departmental and interdepartmental systems such as Finance and Electronic Customs, and electronic business reporting, were being introduced.

The draft noted that Ukraine’s ambitions were still only partly realized, citing low competitiveness and network readiness rankings. It argued that skills shape employment and lifetime earnings and that demand for digital literacy and knowledge-economy skills was rising. Limited ICT access and limited use of ICT in education were weakening digital literacy, and employers reported a mismatch between workers’ skills and their needs.

Conditions for progress

  • Redesign public administration processes so that public bodies work more effectively and at lower cost. Secure support for e-governance initiatives and projects from the country’s leadership.
  • Continue strengthening the legal framework and align it with EU legislation. Make the government accountable for the main ICT priorities, project funding and results.
  • Prepare citizens to interact electronically with government through ICT training and lifelong learning. Foster cooperation among the state, businesses and citizens to support national and individual development.
  • Preserve the government’s ability to select the most effective ICT projects and technologies offered by businesses. Deliver projects that improve public authorities’ work and interactions with citizens and create a favourable investment climate.

Priority tasks

  1. Give a ministry-level executive body the authority to design and implement national ICT policy.
  2. Establish common digital signature standards and implement electronic document exchange among public bodies.
  3. Introduce a national system of information society indicators aligned with EU standards.
  4. Create a single web portal for administrative services and deliver these services electronically to citizens.
  5. Digitize archives, libraries, museums and other cultural collections. Create publicly accessible national systems for library and museum resources and for finding information on Ukrainian history, culture, folk traditions and contemporary art.
  6. Put the law on personal data into effect and ensure that the State Service for Personal Data Protection can operate fully.
  7. Safeguard information sovereignty and security and establish a unified national system to combat cybercrime.
  8. Enable online business registration, reporting and customs clearance.
  9. Support the creation of high-technology companies, export-oriented IT production and software exports.
  10. Launch a national internet access programme that expands Wi-Fi coverage and access in public places, including libraries, schools and community centres.
  11. Equip schools with computers and connect them to the internet to support modern teaching methods. Develop educational software, computer-based assessments and tools to identify gifted pupils.
  12. Set up community computer centres to teach basic digital literacy and use of the internet.
  13. Establish and operate distance education centres, including for vulnerable groups.
  14. Preserve research expertise and workforce capacity in informatization.
  15. Develop partnerships with financial institutions, hardware and software companies and telecommunications operators to subsidize lower-cost internet-connected computers for teachers, pupils, other publicly funded workers and vulnerable groups.
  16. Build Ukraine’s reputation as a high-technology country.
